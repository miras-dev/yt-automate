# What is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

**Idea:** A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who flies to one customer and writes production code on-site until the product actually works for them. Palantir invented the title around 2009; the AI boom made it one of the hottest roles in tech in 2026.

## Scene 1 — Enter the FDE (~25 s)

This engineer isn't at an office.
She's on a plane, flying to one customer.
Her company sells software, but this customer can't make it do what they need.
A normal engineer at HQ would file a feature request.
She's going to sit next to them and write the code herself.
That job has a name now. Forward Deployed Engineer.

*Scene 1 word count: 65*

## Scene 2 — What they actually do (~30 s)

She lands, walks into their office, opens her laptop at an empty desk.
First she asks the hard question: what are you actually trying to do?
Then she starts shipping.
New dashboards. A custom data model. A workflow that didn't exist yesterday.
She writes code on the customer's infrastructure.
When the platform itself gets in the way, she commits a fix back to it.

*Scene 2 word count: 70*

## Scene 3 — Why it's different (~30 s)

Think of a normal software engineer.
One feature, shipped to a million users.
An FDE flips that around.
One customer, a million features.
She owns the whole deployment: scoping it, building it, taking it live, keeping it working.
Palantir, the company that coined the title, once called the job a startup CTO for one customer.
End to end, high stakes, small team.

*Scene 3 word count: 70*

## Scene 4 — Who hires them (~35 s)

The role started at Palantir around 2009.
The name came from military forward deployment: send an engineer to the front line.
For years, barely anyone else used it.
Then the AI boom hit.
Suddenly every big AI company needed someone who could land in an enterprise and make the model actually work.
OpenAI alone had 24 Forward Deployed postings open at once in late 2026.
Anthropic, Google, AWS, Microsoft all added teams.

*Scene 4 word count: 82*

## Scene 5 — The deal (~30 s)

It pays like a founding engineer: around 238 thousand dollars a year on average in the US.
Senior packages at frontier labs clear half a million.
The catch is the lifestyle: up to half the week on a customer's site.
Short timelines. Someone else's data, someone else's rules.
But if you like building something a real person will use on Monday, this is the job.
