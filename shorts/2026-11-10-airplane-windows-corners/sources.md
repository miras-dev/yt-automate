# Sources

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOAC_Flight_781 — BOAC Flight 781 (10 Jan 1954) crashed from metal fatigue in the pressure cabin; investigation led the Comet to replace square windows with rounded ones.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Airways_Flight_201 — SAA Flight 201 (8 Apr 1954) was the second Comet to break up in flight; both losses were traced to fuselage fatigue cracks initiated at sharp cut-outs.
- https://www.fzt.haw-hamburg.de/pers/Scholz/dglr/hh/text_2019_01_24_Comet.pdf — Royal Aeronautical Society Hamburg lecture: cracks grew at window corners under repeated pressurisation; rounded windows distribute the stress and were adopted industry-wide.
- https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/flight/modern/airplane-windows-round.htm — General engineering explanation that sharp corners concentrate stress and circular shapes resist deformation, which is why every commercial aircraft window is rounded.
